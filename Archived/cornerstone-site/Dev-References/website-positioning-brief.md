# Website Positioning Brief — Cornerstone AI

> Handover document for an AI agent or designer editing the Cornerstone AI website. This brief defines the target audience, messaging hierarchy, tone, and page-by-page direction.

---

## Strategic Context

Cornerstone AI is repositioning to serve **financial service providers (FSPs)** — specifically small-to-mid owner-operated practices in accounting, insurance brokerages, and financial advisory. The website must speak directly to these decision-makers and their pain. The cold email campaigns driving traffic are 80% US, 20% South Africa.

**The website should feel like it was built for FSPs** — but not so narrow that a non-FSP business owner who lands on it feels excluded. Think "clearly for financial services" in examples, case studies, and language, while keeping the core proposition universal enough that a logistics firm owner would still see themselves in it.

---

## Target Audience

### Primary: FSP Practice Owners (Decision-Makers)

Three avatars, one shared profile:

| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Role** | Owner, Managing Director, Principal, Key Individual, Senior Partner |
| **Age** | 40-60 |
| **Team size** | 5-20 employees |
| **Revenue** | $500K-$5M (US) / R3M-R30M (SA) |
| **Tech savviness** | Low to moderate. Uses industry-specific software but not experimenting with AI. Knows they should be "doing something with AI" but doesn't know what or where to start. |
| **Buying psychology** | Conservative, risk-averse, trust-driven. Needs to see proof before committing. Responds to data and peer examples, not hype. |
| **Primary emotion** | Overwhelmed. Drowning in admin, compliance, and client work. Too busy to fix the systems that are making them busy. |

### Avatar 1: Accounting Practice Owner
- Drowning in SARS/IRS deadlines, client document chasing, and staff turnover
- Can't find qualified accountants — the pipeline is empty
- Uses Sage/QuickBooks/Xero + CaseWare + spreadsheets. Nothing talks to anything.
- Spends 30%+ of team capacity on rework and chasing documents

### Avatar 2: Insurance Brokerage Owner
- Compliance documentation (Record of Advice) is weeks behind
- Commission reconciliation is manual, error-prone, takes days each month
- Client communication during claims is their biggest complaint source
- Worried about consolidation — "modernize or get acquired"

### Avatar 3: Financial Advisor / RIA Owner
- Paraplanner spends 2 days building one client review pack from 6 different platforms
- FICA/KYC re-verification is a constant admin drain
- Thinking about succession/practice sale — but the practice is too dependent on them personally
- Compliance costs rising 10-20% per year with no end in sight

## Messaging Hierarchy

### Level 1: The Pain (Lead With This)
This is what the visitor feels right now. Validate it immediately.

- "Your admin is eating your billable hours."
- "You're turning away work because your team is maxed out."
- "Compliance documentation is weeks behind — and one inspection could cost you your license."
- "You've tried tools. They sit unused."
- "You can't find qualified staff. The ones you train leave for corporate."

### Level 2: The Shift (Reframe Their Thinking)
Move from pain to possibility. Show them the problem is systemic, not personal.

- "You don't have a people problem. You have a systems problem."
- "The firms that thrive over the next 5 years won't be the ones that hire more. They'll be the ones that automate the right things."
- "Technology isn't the answer. The right technology, implemented for your specific team, is."
- "Every hour your practice depends on you personally reduces its value."

### Level 3: The Solution (How We Work)
Concrete, de-risked, step-by-step. Match their conservative buying psychology.

**Phase 1: Capacity Discovery Sprint (5 days)**
- We embed with your team — interviews, workflow mapping, time analysis
- We identify exactly where hours are being lost and to what
- We produce a data-backed model: can your team's capacity be doubled?
- If we can't prove it, you pay nothing. You keep the findings.

**Phase 2: Capacity Blueprint**
- Multiple automation approaches designed for each bottleneck
- Options presented to the team, not imposed
- Every system justified by hard return calculations — hours saved, errors eliminated, revenue recovered

**Phase 3: Implementation & Adoption (6 weeks)**
- Systems built inside your existing accounts and tools
- White-glove adoption: one-on-one onboarding for every team member
- Weekly check-ins. Unlimited support calls during rollout.
- If your team isn't confidently using the systems by week 6, we keep going at no extra cost.

### Level 4: Proof (Back It Up)
- Specific metrics: "X hours/week recovered," "commission reconciliation from 3 days to 3 hours," "ROA generation from 90 minutes to 15 minutes"
- Case study references (use The Specialists blueprint: R3.6M annual returns identified from R168K implementation — reframe for FSP context)
- Industry statistics: "97% of accounting firms say they use tech inefficiently." "78% of insurance brokerages can't rely on AI in their current software." "Only 10.6% of financial services firms use AI."
- Guarantee language: "If we can't prove 2x capacity, you don't pay."

## Tone & Voice

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Speak plainly. These are smart people who hate jargon. | Don't say "leverage AI to optimize synergies" |
| Use their industry language: "Record of Advice," "commission reconciliation," "client onboarding," "FICA files" | Don't use generic tech language: "digital transformation," "cutting-edge solutions," "disruptive innovation" |
| Be direct and confident. They respect competence. | Don't be salesy or pushy. They sell for a living — they'll see through it. |
| Show you understand their specific world | Don't be vague about what you actually do |
| Acknowledge the risk they feel | Don't dismiss their caution as "resistance to change" |
| Be warm but professional | Don't be overly casual or use emojis |

### Voice Examples

**Good:** "Your paraplanner shouldn't need two days to build one client review pack. That's not a people problem — it's a systems problem. We fix the systems."

**Bad:** "Leverage our cutting-edge AI solutions to transform your practice management workflows and unlock unprecedented operational efficiencies."

**Good:** "We work with accounting firms, insurance brokerages, and financial advisory practices. Our clients typically get 15-25 hours per week back within 6 weeks."

**Bad:** "We help businesses of all sizes across all industries implement revolutionary AI automation technology."

## Design Direction (For the Designer/Agent)

- **Clean, professional, trustworthy.** Think law firm website meets modern SaaS — not startup-flashy, not corporate-boring.
- **Color palette:** Whatever the current brand is, ensure it communicates trust, stability, competence. Avoid bright/loud colors that feel like a tech startup.
- **Typography:** Clean, readable. No decorative fonts. These are people who read financial statements.
- **Imagery:** If using photos, show real office environments (not stock photos of people pointing at screens). Ideally, show small teams in professional settings.
- **Mobile-first:** Insurance brokers check email on their phones. The site must be fast and readable on mobile.

## What NOT to Do

- Don't lead with "AI" in every headline. These buyers are skeptical of AI hype. Lead with outcomes (hours saved, capacity gained, compliance solved) and let AI be the method, not the message.
- Don't use the word "disrupt" or "revolutionize." These people value stability.
- Don't show pricing tiers like a SaaS product. This is consultative, custom engagement. Show the process and the guarantee, not a pricing grid.
- Don't ignore the team adoption angle. The #1 unspoken fear is "we'll pay for this and nobody will use it." The Team Adoption Guarantee must be prominent.
- Don't make the site feel like it's for enterprises. These are 5-20 person firms. The site should feel accessible, not intimidating.
